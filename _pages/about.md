For as long as I remember, I have liked creating things. Whether it was a computer game when I was ten, a projection tool for displaying screens on water vapor when I was in junior high, or an hour-long feature film in high school, I have always been drawn to making ideas real.

I am currently a PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Rochester, working at the intersection of human-computer interaction, human-AI interaction, AI literacy, educational technology, and interactive systems. My research focuses on designing, building, and evaluating AI-driven learning experiences, including embodied conversational agents, Minecraft-based AI literacy tools, educational games, and interactive prototypes.

I studied Computer Engineering at Sharif University of Technology to build the technical foundation for creating impactful software. Along the way, I also contributed to SSC, co-founded the educational NGO Rasta, taught programming and creative writing at NODET schools, published short stories, directed podcasts and radio plays, and designed the exhibition and curriculum for Iran's Computer Museum.

I later studied Cinema Studies at Soore University of Arts, which strengthened my interest in storytelling, media, and the cultural meaning of technology. Across research, product work, software development, game design, and filmmaking, my goal has remained the same: to create meaningful interactive experiences that combine technical depth, human-centered design, and imagination.
